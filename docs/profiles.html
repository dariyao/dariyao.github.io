Profile Writing & Editing · VIDA Select

Other people,
on paper.

At VIDA Select I wrote personality-driven profiles for premium clients, then moved into editorial leadership, editing other writers’ work.

This guide outlines an approach to writing a profile that feels specific, natural and recognisably human.

The Editorial Lens

Specific enough
to picture.

Natural enough
to say aloud.

True enough
to recognise.

Keep the person in the prose.

The Approach

Listen closely.
Write specifically.
Edit with care.

01

Start with the Person

Look for the details that someone would actually notice in conversation: a ritual, an enthusiasm, a surprising preference. Ask follow-up questions until you have something more revealing than a list of interests.

02

Show the Trait

“Adventurous” tells a reader very little. A concrete detail gives them something to picture and respond to. Choose details that reveal a quality without announcing it.

03

Keep Their Voice

Polish should make a person easier to hear. Read the draft aloud, remove expressions they would never use, and keep the humour at a level that feels natural to them.

04

Give the Reader a Way In

A good profile offers openings for a conversation. A particular interest or gentle question gives someone a better starting point than a generic invitation to get in touch.

05

Edit for Recognition

Check accuracy, rhythm and repetition. Then ask whether the person would recognise themselves in the result. A persuasive profile should create interest without promising a personality that is not there.

Good editing makes someone
easier to hear.
