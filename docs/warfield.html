Feature · The Spill Magazine · 21 July 2022

“I like the idea of being included in a part of something, more than making myself the focal point”

A conversation with Justin Warfield of She Wants Revenge

By Dariya Ostrovska · Main photo: Kateryna Debkaliuk

What I noticed

He described writing “Everything To Me” as following a map he already had in his head. So I built the piece around storytelling as his method, and gave him the headline.

A background in writing both non-fiction and fiction, I wrote, had shaped his method “to reflect a novelist, rather than someone writing in a personal diary.”

“Everything To Me” was commissioned for an upcoming 25th anniversary. Warfield doesn’t say the word. The lyric is “a quarter of a century with you”, which carries more intention, “likely because the only way to understand something is through comparison — a quarter to a century.”

Early She Wants Revenge borrowed from Depeche Mode, The Smiths and New Order, and followed rules: guitars with reverb, but no fuzz or heavy distortion.

“It was a way to have restraint.”

By Valleyheart the rules were gone. For the next record, he told me, there would be one: don’t repeat ourselves, and don’t try to sound like something else.

Two hiatuses meant getting back together took a hard look at the past first.

“She Wants Revenge should not cease to be because two people can’t do the simple thing of getting along.”

On the lyrics for the next record, he wasn’t worried. Once he heard the music, he said, he’d know what the stories were supposed to be.
